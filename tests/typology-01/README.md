# typology-01: how widespread is each of Apupiwe's grammatical features?

**Question** (author, 2026-11-19, reframing the goal): which of Apupiwe's grammatical features are what the world's languages have converged on, which are common, which are regional, and which have no natural precedent? Features nothing widespread supports must earn their place; this test supplies the data for that, feature by feature. It reports and classifies only. It recommends no change.

**Answer in one line:** of 115 features, 41 are converged, 52 common, 15 regional and 7 invented (counts below). Each part of the head-initial word order is common, but the whole bundle at once is regional (W14). So is having no inflection at all (M01).

## Method

1. **Features.** Every grammatical feature in `docs/language.md` (generated 2026-11-19, last commit `e0512e4`), one row each with its rule id, in [`features.psv`](features.psv). Conventions that only carry a culture or a standard (clock, ISO dates, weekday numbers, the ICAO spelling alphabet, metric units, loan adaptation, chat notation) aren't typological features, so they're left out. Pure phonetic detail is left out too (syllable timing, stress without length). Lexicon-design rules are one row (S20).
2. **Data.** Grambank CLDF v1.0.3 (GitHub `grambank/grambank`, 2023-04-19; Skirgård et al. 2023), 2,393 language-level lects, with the 70 dialect rows dropped. WALS CLDF v2020.6 (GitHub `cldf-datasets/wals`, 2026-09-28; Dryer & Haspelmath eds. 2013), 2,660 languages. Both were downloaded to `~/work/scratch/typology-01/dl/` (not in the repo). Zenodo also lists a "Grambank - clld app v2026.11" record; that is the web app, and I didn't check whether it carries newer data. The WALS chapter texts quoted here are from the CLDF release's `docs/` folder.
3. **Measures.** Each feature maps to one or more database features, written `DB:PARAM=VALUES`, with `&` joining conditions (for example "no case" = `GB070=0&GB071=0&GB072=0&GB073=0`). [`compute.py`](compute.py) computes, per measure:
   - **share**: languages with the value, divided by languages coded for every parameter in the measure ("?" excluded);
   - **family-balanced share**: for each family, the share of its languages; then the mean over families. Grambank families are its `Family_level_ID`. In WALS each language of the "other" group counts alone. Isolates count as one-language families, which weights them heavily (Grambank has about 100);
   - **macro-areas**: of the six (Africa, Eurasia, Papunesia, Australia, North America, South America), how many have at least one such language, and how many have it in at least 10% of their coded languages. `results.tsv` also gives the share in each area.
4. **Primary measure.** The first measure in a row is the closest match. Where Grambank and WALS match equally well, Grambank comes first, as the larger sample.
5. **Class**, from the primary measure's numbers unless the row says otherwise:
   - **converged**: family-balanced share ≥ 50%;
   - **common**: 10–50% and at ≥ 10% in at least three macro-areas;
   - **regional**: below that;
   - **invented**: no language found.

   Rows without a database feature are classed by hand from a sourced statement, marked in the note as **sourced** (I read the quoted text in the source), **inferred** or **recalled, unverified**. They are less certain than the computed rows.
6. **Match quality.** **good** means the same category. **fair** means close, with the mismatch named in the note. **poor** means it only covers part of the feature. **none** means no database feature exists.

Rerun: `python3 -I tests/typology-01/compute.py GRAMBANK/cldf WALS/cldf`. It rewrites `results.tsv` and the table and counts below.

## Caveats

- **The thresholds are the brief's own, applied mechanically.** Several features sit on a line:
  - N09 ordinals: family-balanced share 9.98%;
  - V39 no morphological causative: 9.1%;
  - N16 diminutive: 51%;
  - W07 noun-numeral: 49%;
  - W11 'all' after the noun: 47%.
- **The family-balanced share leans towards what isolates and small families do.** Raw shares are given beside it.
- **WALS and Grambank sometimes disagree.**
  - No articles: WALS 37A gives 32%, Grambank 60%. WALS counts a demonstrative used as an article as an article.
  - Double objects: WALS 105A gives 22% for the main strategy; Grambank GB105 gives 57% for "can".
  - Both figures are in the table.
- **Grambank mostly asks "can …?"; WALS mostly asks "what is the dominant …?".** So Grambank shares run higher for optional strategies.
- **A feature's prevalence is not that of Apupiwe's whole version.** The note says where Apupiwe goes further than the natural pattern (for example evidentials: three-way and optional; obviation: extended to things; reduplication: fixed meanings for doubled grammar words).
- **Several web sources were refused by their sites:** Güldemann's papers (HU Berlin bot check), De Gruyter, pure.mpg.de, HAL and DOAJ. Those statements are marked recalled. Downloads from the sources that were read are in `~/work/scratch/typology-01/web-a/` and `web-b/`.

## Regional and invented features

**Regional** (mainly one or two macro-areas, or under 10% of families):
- **M01 no inflection at all** (Grambank strict proxy): 4% of languages, 1% family-balanced, 12% in Eurasia (Mainland Southeast Asia) and at most 2% elsewhere. WALS 26A "little affixation" alone comes out common (15%).
- **W14 the full head-initial bundle**:
  - covers SVO, prepositions, N-Adj, N-Dem, N-Num, N-Gen and N-Rel together;
  - 11% of languages, 3% family-balanced;
  - 26% in Africa, 10% in Papunesia, 6% in Eurasia, none in Australia or the Americas.
- **N07 'you all'/'they' as person word + plural word** (WALS 35A, fair match): 7%.
- **N09 ordinals regularly from every cardinal, including 'one'** (WALS 53A): 13%, 10% family-balanced (borderline). The same marker also forms labels; that part has no data.
- **N12 indefinites built on generic nouns** (WALS 46A): 26% of languages, but almost all in Africa (93%) and Papunesia (76%).
- **N19 the closer kiyu**: relative-clause brackets are attested in Ewe and Nkami (Kwa) (sourced), and Tok Pisin (recalled). No count exists. Apupiwe's single closer for relative clauses, names, topics and derived words goes beyond them.
- **S05 aspiration and voicing together as a double cue**: recalled, mainly Germanic.
- **V14 commissive du**: "this cross-linguistically rare type" (Korean promissive; Pak, Portner & Zanuttini 2008).
- **V26 politeness particle wu**: no count found. Addressee-politeness marking is "a handful" of languages (Wang); free particles are recalled as mainly East and Southeast Asian.
- **V30 obviation mon/tun, bun/fin**: in the narrow sense only Algonquian and Kutenai (Dryer; Bliss 2017). Apupiwe also extends it to things.
- **V31 logophoric son** (Grambank GB167): 7% of languages; 20% in Africa, at most 5% elsewhere.
- **V32 generic human pronoun dan**: an areal feature of Standard Average European (Giacalone Ramat & Sansò 2007).
- **V39 no morphological or compound causative** (WALS 111A "neither"): 7%. It follows from M01.
- **V54 class-free roots**: fully flexible ("type 1") are only Samoan and Tagalog in a 50-language sample, plus Mundari as 1/2 (Hengeveld, Rijkhoff & Siewierska 2004). Even these are contested (Evans & Osada 2005).
- **V55 name marker fo**: proprial articles are concentrated in Austronesian (Stolz & Levkovych). No world count.

**Invented** (no natural precedent found):
- **S20** the lexicon rules against accent mergers and digit-like onsets. These are a design constraint, not grammar.
- **N17** tan, a marker for a modifier of a modifier ("dark-red").
- **V13** the advice particle fon.
- **V17** the not-literal (irony) particle genso. No grammatical irony marker was found anywhere; Cypriot-Greek *siga* is lexical.
- **V48** confidence as a number of sixths, pe + digit. Epistemic marking itself is universal.
- **V49** counterfactual conditionals as zero confidence (sin pe mu). Counterfactual marking is common; doing it this way is Apupiwe's own.
- **V56** a multi-sentence quote bracketed by the same word, kenlin … kenlin.

Notable within the larger classes: evidentials (V47) are **common** but concentrated in the Americas (41% of South American languages). Apupiwe's version is optional and three-way, the "B1" type (Aikhenvald 2004). The confidence scale V48 is **invented**, though every language has some epistemic marker. The warning particle yo (V15) is **common** but only a fair match: apprehensives mark a feared event ("lest"), not a warning speech act.
